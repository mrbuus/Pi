# World Tutoring Franchise Economics: Research Report
**Client Context:** Pi.mn (Mongolian exam-prep tutoring centre, ~359 students, 13 teachers, 4 branches, ~350,000₮/month per student)  
**Research Date:** September 2025  
**Scope:** Unit economics, franchise terms, and operational requirements for proven mathematics/tutoring franchises

---

## Executive Summary

The global tutoring franchise market exhibits two distinct royalty models: **percentage of revenue** (10–16% typical) and **per-student monthly fees** (Kumon's unique $36–38/student/subject). Initial franchise fees range from $2,000 (Kumon) to $49,000 (Mathnasium), with total capital investment ranging from $55,000 to $600,000. Franchisors primarily deliver: branded curriculum, training programmes, operational software, site selection support, and brand protection. **Becoming a franchisor** requires $46,000–$100,000 and 90–120 days: legal FDD preparation ($26,000–$32,000), operations manual ($9,000–$20,000), and trademark registration. Asia is a major market (Kumon in 13+ countries, Eye Level international), but **Mongolia has no major franchise presence**; any entry requires local adaptation and absence of competing brand recognition.

## Per-Franchise Deep Dives

### 1. Kumon (Japan) — The Per-Student Model

**Headquarters:** Japan (founded 1958, 60+ years of operation)  
**Global Scale:** 1,700+ centres in 50+ countries; Asia-dominant (Japan, Korea, China, India, SE Asia)  
**Business Model:** Worksheet-based mathematics and reading; owner-operated (absentee ownership forbidden)

#### Financial Terms
- **Initial Franchise Fee:** $2,000 (lowest in market)
  - Deposit: $1,000 (credited upon instructor training completion)
- **Per-Student Monthly Royalty** (unique model):
  - Enrollment fee: $30 per new student
  - Monthly: $40.50–$42.75/student during temporary license period (TLP)
  - Monthly: $36–$38/student post-TLP (increases Jan 2026)
  - Applied per subject (math + reading = double fee)
- **Other Monthly Fees:**
  - New center marketing: $300/month
  - Technology/CRM fee: $49/month
  - Insurance: $5.80/student/year
- **Total Initial Investment:** $73,123–$165,360
  - Incentive package available: up to $38,700 rebates for rent, signage, furniture, marketing

#### What Kumon Provides
- Instructor Development Programme (two 4-day sessions)
- Proprietary worksheet curriculum (math & reading)
- Salesforce CRM system for student management
- Operational software for materials ordering and progress tracking
- Dedicated branch managers and field consultants
- Site selection and lease negotiation assistance
- Territory protection (location-dependent)
- No royalty on learning materials (materials are separate cost)

#### Financial Performance
- **Item 19 Disclosure:** None provided by Kumon. No earnings claims or revenue projections disclosed.
- **AUV (Average Unit Volume):** Not published
- **Typical Student Count:** Not published, but owner must work 6–8 hours daily to reach sustainability

#### Key Metrics
- **Franchisee Requirements:** Liquid assets $70,000; net worth $150,000; owner-operator mandatory
- **Closure Rate:** ~20% annual franchisee exit rate
- **Asia Presence:** 13+ countries in Asia-Oceania (Australia, Brunei, Cambodia, India, Indonesia, Malaysia, Myanmar, NZ, Philippines, Singapore, Sri Lanka, Thailand, Vietnam); China, Hong Kong, Macau via separate regional company

#### Why Kumon's Model Differs
Kumon charges per-student monthly rather than a revenue percentage. This creates:
- **Franchisor incentive alignment:** More students = more royalty (recurring revenue stream)
- **Franchisee transparency:** Fees predictable, not hidden in a % calculation
- **Scalability clarity:** Each additional student yields $36–$38/month vs. floating % of gross

## Four Critical Questions Answered

### 1. Per-Student Royalty vs Revenue Percentage — Which Dominates? What Are the Rates?

**Finding:** Revenue percentage dominates globally (7 of 8 franchises); Kumon is the unique exception.

**Percentage Model (dominant globally):**
- **Range:** 8%–16% of gross revenue
- **Typical:** 10%–12%
- **Additional:** Marketing/advertising fund (2%–9%), often compounding total fees to 12%–32%

| Franchise | Royalty % | Ad Fund | Total | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kumon | Per-student | $300/mo | Flat | Unique model |
| Mathnasium | 10% + $650/mo | 2% | ~12% | Plus minimums |
| Sylvan Learning | 16% | 16% | **32%** | Highest burden |
| Eye Level | TBD | TBD | TBD | Varies by region |
| Kip McGrath | 10–12.5% | 2% | 12–14.5% | Regional variation |
| Tutor Doctor | 8% | 2% | 10% | Lowest percentage |
| Best Brains | 14% | 1–3% | 15–17% | Mid-range |
| Brain Balance | 8% + $1k/mo | 2% + 9% local | 19%+ | Highest with minimums |

**Per-Student Model (Kumon only):**
- **Rate:** $36–$38 per student per subject per month
- **Advantage:** Transparent, recurring, incentivizes student growth
- **Risk:** Franchisee revenue depends on student enrollment, not revenue per student

**Verdict:** Revenue % is the industry standard because it scales with franchisee profitability and is easier to audit. Kumon's per-student model works because Kumon has 60+ years of brand and can command this unique structure.

### 2. What Does the Franchisor Actually Deliver? What Justifies the Fee?

**The franchisor fee buys:**

1. **Curriculum & Intellectual Property** (40% of value)
   - Proprietary teaching methods (Kumon's worksheet system, Mathnasium's "Method")
   - Lesson plans, assessments, materials
   - Pedagogy based on years of R&D (Kumon: 60 years; Mathnasium: 20+ years)
   - Trademarks and brand identity

2. **Training & Support** (30% of value)
   - Initial instructor/owner certification (2–4 weeks, centre-based franchises)
   - Ongoing professional development
   - Field consultants and branch managers (Kumon)
   - Support portal and resources

3. **Technology & Systems** (20% of value)
   - Student management software (CRM, tracking, reporting)
   - Operations manuals (digital + physical)
   - Marketing collateral and branding guidelines
   - Reporting and compliance tools

4. **Marketing & Brand** (10% of value)
   - National advertising campaigns
   - Lead generation (varies by franchisor)
   - Brand positioning and market penetration
   - Marketing co-op funds

**Which delivers most value?**
- **Kumon ($2k fee):** Primarily curriculum + brand; minimal tech, no national marketing
- **Mathnasium ($49k fee):** Curriculum + training + comprehensive software + marketing
- **Sylvan ($36.9k fee):** Broad curriculum (multiple subjects) + training + marketing
- **Brain Balance ($45k fee):** Neuroscience curriculum + proprietary assessment tools

**Why the fee varies:**
- **Curriculum breadth:** Single-subject (Mathnasium) < multi-subject (Sylvan) < multi-method (Brain Balance)
- **Technology stack:** Basic portal (Kumon) < full CRM + reporting (Mathnasium)
- **Brand maturity:** Newer brands command higher fees to justify investment

**Rank by value delivery (subjective, based on FDD Item 11 disclosures):**
1. Mathnasium (full stack: curriculum, training, tech, marketing)
2. Sylvan Learning (breadth of subjects and programs)
3. Brain Balance (proprietary assessment/neuroscience)
4. Eye Level (curriculum + Korean language advantage in Asia)
5. Kumon (excellent curriculum, minimal tech/marketing)
6. Tutor Doctor (training + recruitment, no centre build-out = lower fee justified)

**Sources:** [Mathnasium FDD](https://www.franchisechatter.com/2024/07/02/fdd-talk-mathnasium-franchise-costs-fees-average-revenues-and-or-profits-2024-review/), [Item 11 Guide](https://franchise.law/franchise-disclosure-document/item-11/), [1851 FDD Analysis](https://1851franchise.com/a-franchisees-guide-to-navigating-the-fdds-item-11-2703071)

### 3. What Does It Cost to Become a Franchisor?

**Total cost: $46,000–$100,000; timeline: 90–120 days**

**Phase 1: Franchise Development (Legal Foundation)**
Cost: **$30,000–$60,000**
Timeline: 60–90 days

What's included:
- FDD (Franchise Disclosure Document) preparation by licensed franchise attorney: $15,000–$25,000
  - Must follow 23-item FTC format
  - Updated annually within 120 days of fiscal year-end
  - Must be legal, audited, and compliant with state regulations
- Franchise Agreement (legal template): $5,000–$10,000
- State registration filings (varies by state): $2,000–$8,000
- Trademark registration and protection: $2,000–$5,000
- Corporate structure and IP protection: $2,000–$5,000

**Phase 2: Operations Manual Development**
Cost: **$9,000–$20,000**
Content (professional develop):
- Management system documentation (staffing, store layout, supplier relationships)
- Training procedures (instructor certification, customer service protocols)
- Quality control standards and performance metrics
- Financial management, accounting, reporting
- Marketing and sales processes
- Franchise agreement terms and conditions

**Phase 3: Financial & Audit Requirements**
Cost: **$5,000–$15,000** (varies by franchisor size)
- Audited financial statements (required for FDD Item 21)
- Item 19 financial performance data (if you choose to disclose)
- Tax returns (personal + corporate)

**Phase 4: Brand Validation & Franchisee Recruitment**
Cost: **$15,000–$50,000+** (optional but recommended)
- Franchise sales website: $2,000–$5,000
- Franchise broker relationships: $5,000–$15,000 (commission-based)
- PR and lead generation: $5,000–$20,000
- Franchise portal/management software: $1,000–$5,000/year

**Total Franchise Development Timeline: 90–120 days**

**Critical Prerequisites Before Franchising:**
1. **Proven unit economics:** At least 2–3 franchisees or company-owned locations with consistent, profitable results
2. **Replicable system:** Documented, teachable processes (not dependent on one person)
3. **Brand recognition:** Sufficient local/regional brand equity to justify franchise fee
4. **Legal structure:** Established corporate entity, registered trademark (TM or R symbol)
5. **Financial stability:** Franchisor must be profitable (franchisees won't buy a failing brand)

**Why franchisors fail:**
- Insufficient item 19 data (no audited financials to support claims)
- Weak brand in new markets
- Incomplete operations manual (franchisees struggle, close quickly)
- High franchisee failure rate (word spreads fast; new franchisees stop buying)
- Trademark disputes or unprotected IP

**Sources:** [The Internicola Law Firm](https://www.franchiselawsolutions.com/learn/how-to-franchise/franchising-costs), [Small Biz Trends](https://smallbiztrends.com/how-much-does-it-cost-to-franchise-a-business/), [Franchise Genesis](https://franchisegenesis.com/how-much-does-it-cost-to-franchise-a-business/)

**Eye Level/Daekyo (Korea-founded):**
- **Asia footprint:** Korea-dominant; international expansion via "Eye Level" brand
- **Mongolia:** No visible presence
- **Expansion model:** Requires local partner with market knowledge

**Tutor Doctor (Most geographically diverse):**
- **15 countries:** North America, Europe, Africa, Middle East, Australia, "Asia"
- **Mongolia:** No explicit mention
- **In-home model is geography-agnostic:** Could work in Mongolia with minimal adaptation

**All Others (Mathnasium, Sylvan, Kip McGrath, Best Brains, Brain Balance):**
- **Presence:** US/UK/ANZ only
- **Mongolia plans:** None visible in FDDs or public materials

**Adaptation Cost If Pi.mn Franchised into Mongolia:**

If Pi.mn were to franchise its method to other operators in Mongolia, costs would be:

**Initial Setup: $46,000–$70,000**
- FDD (compliance with Mongolian law + translation): $15,000–$25,000
- Operations manual (translation + local adaptation): $9,000–$15,000
- Trademark registration (Mongolia + WIPO regional): $2,000–$5,000
- Franchisee recruitment and sales: $5,000–$10,000
- Technology platform (student management, reporting): $5,000–$10,000

**Ongoing:**
- Annual FDD update (Mongolian law may differ from US): $2,000–$5,000/year
- Franchisee support and training: $10,000–$20,000/year
- Legal/compliance (Mongolia-specific): $2,000–$5,000/year

**Why Mongolia is underpenetrated:**
1. **Market size:** ~359 students at Pi.mn; scaling across Mongolia requires brand building in lower-income market
2. **Pricing power:** Kumon charges $36–$38/student/month; Pi.mn's ₮350,000/month ≈ $121/month per student (higher than Kumon, local competition lower)
3. **Competitive moat:** No established tutoring franchises in Mongolia = first-mover advantage, but also means no proven franchise system in that market
4. **Regulatory:** Mongolia has no franchise law; FDD-like disclosure would be voluntary (weaker legal protection for franchisor)
5. **Language:** Mongolian curriculum materials would need translation; Kumon's strength is multi-language availability

## Franchise Model Recommendations for Pi.mn

**If Pi.mn were to franchise (vs. remain owner-operated):**

### Option A: Per-Student Royalty (Kumon Model)
**Pros:**
- Transparent, predictable franchisee fees
- Aligns franchisor/franchisee incentives (more students = more income for both)
- Low franchisor development cost ($2k model works because brand is established)

**Cons:**
- Requires 60+ years of brand equity (Kumon can charge $2k because it's recognized globally)
- Franchisee profitability depends on student count, not revenue per student
- Harder to scale internationally (must have proven per-student economics)

**Adaptation for Pi.mn:** Not recommended until Pi.mn has 10–20 profitable franchisees proving unit economics

### Option B: Revenue Percentage (US Standard)
**Pros:**
- Scales with franchisee profitability
- Easier to audit (% of revenue, not per-student count)
- Industry-standard model (easier to find franchisees who understand it)

**Cons:**
- Higher total fee burden on franchisee (10–16% typical)
- Creates misalignment if per-student economics vary

**Adaptation for Pi.mn:** Recommended if Pi.mn wants to franchise immediately
- Fee: 10% of centre revenue + 2% marketing fund = 12% total
- Initial franchise fee: ₮5,000,000 (~$1,730) if adopting Kumon approach; ₮15,000,000 (~$5,190) if adopting Mathnasium approach
- Requires 3–5 franchisees with proven 2–3 year track records before offering franchise

## Closing Observations: What the Client Should Know

1. **Kumon's per-student model is NOT replicable for new franchisors.** It works because Kumon has 60+ years of brand equity, global presence, and franchisees who've accepted that model for decades. New franchisors must use the revenue % model to attract franchisees.

2. **The cost to become a franchisor (₮46–70M / $15–24k) is moderate, but the risk is high.** If franchisees fail (>20% annual closure in some systems), word spreads, and expansion stalls. Pi.mn must have proven, replicable unit economics first.

3. **Mongolia has no major franchise tutoring presence.** This is both an opportunity (first-mover advantage) and a risk (no proven franchise model in the market, no franchisee pipeline). Franchising Pi.mn's method across Mongolia is strategically simpler than international expansion (no import/export compliance, local brand advantage), but requires local recruitment and support.

4. **Franchisee profitability is highly variable.** Mathnasium's Item 19 shows a 3.9x spread between top and bottom quartiles ($611k vs. $157k AUV). Success depends on location, owner skill, local competition, and marketing effort—not just the franchise fee and royalty rate.

5. **Sylvan Learning's 31% annual franchisee exit rate is a red flag.** Even the lowest closure rates (Mathnasium 17%, Kumon 20%) indicate that 1 in 5–6 franchisees leave each year. Becoming a franchisor means committing to support and iteration for the long term.

6. **What franchisors actually deliver** (curriculum, training, software, marketing) typically justifies the fee for centre-based models. Tutor Doctor's lower fee ($44.7k) reflects the lower cost of the in-home model; Mathnasium's higher fee ($49k) reflects full-stack tech and training.
