## 2. DOCUMENTING YOUR TEACHING METHOD
### Making Tacit Knowledge Explicit and Transferable

Your teaching method is a competitive advantage but also your biggest franchising risk. It lives partly in experienced teachers' heads—the judgment about problem difficulty, how to recognize when students are struggling, pacing decisions. Here is how to extract and document it.

### The Pi.mn Teaching Method as a Case Study

**Current informal method:**
1. Teacher lectures theory at the board
2. Teacher works example problems, easy → hard, chosen to cover the hard items of that day's test
3. Students sit a paper test
4. At 5–10 minutes in, an assistant circulates helping whoever is stuck
5. Teacher marks right/wrong while walking the room (real-time assessment)
6. Students who finish and correct their errors may leave
7. Phones confiscated; students self-report results in the evening
8. Weak classes get the hardest problems REMOVED from the test
9. Leave is deliberately rare

### How to Document This Systematically

#### Document 1: Lesson Planning Protocol (for the operations manual)

Create a step-by-step checklist that a newly-trained teacher must follow to prepare a lesson:

**Example sections:**
- **Problem Selection Logic:** Describe the algorithm. 
  - "For today's test topic X, identify 3–4 common student errors from past tests. Select example problems that each isolate one error. Order them: easiest to hardest. The hardest problem should be 1–2 levels above the target difficulty."
  - Provide 2–3 worked examples of this selection for different topics.
  
- **Lecture Preparation:** How much time for theory vs. practice? (Your model seems to be ~20 min theory, 30 min examples.)
  
- **Test Difficulty Calibration:** 
  - "If >50% of students in a cohort scored <60% on the last test in this topic, reduce the hardest 2 problems to medium difficulty, or remove them entirely."
  - Document the trigger thresholds (which metrics trigger what changes).
  
- **Marking Protocol:** Define how to mark "efficiently while circulating."
  - Tick/cross only, no partial credit? 
  - Time limit per problem set?
  - Record results how (whiteboard, notebook)?

#### Document 2: Teacher Competency Assessment (for trainer certification)

Since you can't fully codify teacher judgment, you must certify teachers through **mentorship, observation, and practice:**

**Three-phase model (SECI framework):**

1. **Socialization (Learning by Doing):** New teacher shadows an experienced teacher for 5–10 lessons, observes problem selection, pacing, and real-time troubleshooting. New teacher co-marks with experienced teacher.

2. **Externalization (Making Tacit Explicit):** Experienced teacher explains aloud during shadowing: *"I'm removing this problem because...because the cohort showed weakness in X."* Document these explanations in a "Reasoning Guide" (e.g., "When to Simplify a Test").

3. **Combination (Practice & Feedback):** New teacher delivers 3–5 lessons under observation. Trainer uses a **Lesson Observation Checklist** to assess:
   - [ ] Problem set covers all target concepts
   - [ ] Problems are ordered easy → hard
   - [ ] Teacher responds appropriately when students are stuck (checks understanding, does not give answer)
   - [ ] Marking is legible and prompt
   - [ ] Lesson timing is appropriate (theory 20–25 min, examples 30–35 min, test ~30 min)
   - [ ] Teacher adjusts difficulty mid-week based on previous results

4. **Internalization (Independence):** Teacher delivers 2 weeks of independent lessons. Supervisor reviews test outcomes and lesson plans. If student outcomes are within ±5% of network average, teacher is certified.

**Time required:** 2–3 weeks per new teacher.

#### Document 3: Variation by Cohort Strength (Quality Control)

Document your "weak classes get hardest problems removed" rule:

**Example protocol:**
- **Tuesday morning cohort (beginning of week):** If cohort average was <55% on this topic last week, remove the 2–3 hardest problems and extend the medium-difficulty section.
- **Thursday afternoon cohort (late week):** If >75% of students scored >80%, add harder problems.
- **Trigger threshold:** Document the exact data (average score, % at threshold) that triggers each rule.

Store this in a **Cohort Adaptation Guide** with examples of past cohorts and how the test was adjusted.

#### Document 4: The Evening Self-Report System (Operational Procedure)

Document how phones-confiscated + evening reporting works:

- **Procedure:** Teachers hand over phone at start of test. After test, students self-report score to a spreadsheet (paper or online).
- **Validation:** Teachers check self-reports against marked papers and flag discrepancies.
- **Why it works:** Creates accountability without surveillance; students remember their errors and can share results with parents.
- **Failure mode:** If students report falsely, audit the system (spot-check 10% of reports) and re-brief students on honesty.

**Franchisees must replicate this exactly.** Document it as a procedure, not a suggestion.

---

### The Honest Truth About Teaching Method Transfer

**What transfers:** Procedures, problem selection logic, test protocols, marking systems, cohort adaptation rules.

**What does not fully transfer:** The judgment that comes from 3–5 years of experience—knowing at a glance whether a student has a conceptual gap or careless error, pacing decisions when a topic is "not landing" with a group, recognizing when a student is disengaged vs. struggling.

**Your mitigation:** 
- Require 2–3 weeks of in-person mentoring for every new teacher before they teach independently.
- Mandate annual refresher training (1–2 days/year) where franchise managers visit a training center to observe and receive live feedback.
- Use student outcome data (cohort pass rates, score distributions) as a leading indicator of teacher quality; if a location's cohort average drops >10% YoY, trigger retraining.

## 3. TEACHER TRAINING AND CERTIFICATION

### Duration and Cost

**Initial training:**
- **Duration:** 2–3 weeks of supervised practice (alongside formal classroom delivery) before certification.
- **Format:** Mostly on-the-job mentoring + 1–2 days of theoretical instruction (exam format, your teaching philosophy, system procedures).
- **Trainer requirement:** One senior teacher per franchise location must be trained as a trainer (additional 1 week).

**Cost:**
- **Who pays:** Franchisee is responsible for teacher salaries during training. Franchisor (Pi.mn) can offer train-the-trainer workshops (3–5 days/year) for $2,000–$5,000/franchisee to cover new best practices and system updates.
- **Ongoing:** Budget $100–$300/teacher/year for annual refresher training and continuing education.

### Competency Verification

**Before a teacher can independently deliver tests:**
- [ ] Observation of 5 lessons by a certified trainer using the Lesson Observation Checklist.
- [ ] Student outcome data from 2 weeks of solo teaching is within ±5% of network average for the same student cohort.
- [ ] Trainer sign-off on a "Teacher Certification Form" (template in operations manual).

**When a teacher underperforms:**
- [ ] Student cohort average drops >10% YoY → trigger re-observation and retraining.
- [ ] If average is still low after retraining, franchisee may need to reassign the teacher or (in severe cases) PI.mn provides a loan-out of a senior teacher for 2–4 weeks.

## 4. QUALITY CONTROL MECHANISMS

### Table: Effectiveness and Cost

| Mechanism | Description | Effectiveness | Cost | Frequency |
|-----------|-----------|---|---|---|
| **Scheduled Audits (Announced)** | Franchisor/QA team visits location, reviews operations manual compliance, student records, teacher certifications | Medium | $1,500–$3,000/audit | Quarterly (4x/year) |
| **Unannounced Audits** | Same as above but without notice; catches real-time behavior vs. "dress rehearsal" compliance | High | $2,000–$4,000/audit | 1–2x/year per location |
| **Student Outcome Tracking** | Collect test scores by cohort/teacher; compare to network average; flag locations >10% below average | High | $500–$1,000/year (software + data entry) | Weekly or fortnightly |
| **Mystery Shopper / Test Observation** | External observer attends a lesson unannounced and completes the Lesson Observation Checklist | High | $1,000–$2,000/visit | 1x/year per location |
| **Franchisee Scorecard** | Monthly/quarterly report card: enrollment trends, test pass rates, teacher retention, parent satisfaction NPS, financial health | High | $200–$500/month (data compilation) | Monthly |
| **Annual In-Person Training** | Franchisee manager + 1 senior teacher attend franchisor workshop to observe best practices, get live feedback | Medium-High | $3,000–$6,000/franchisee/year (travel + event fees) | Annually |
| **Student/Parent Feedback** | NPS or satisfaction survey; focus on "would you recommend" and "teacher quality"; triangulate with outcome data | Medium | $300–$800/year (survey platform) | Quarterly |

**Cost summary per franchisee per year:** ~$12,000–$20,000 in direct franchisor QA costs (audits + training + data management), plus ~$500–$1,000/month for franchisee to implement internal tracking.

**Why multiple mechanisms matter:** Audits catch compliance; student outcomes reveal effectiveness; mystery shoppers identify what students actually experience vs. what the manual says should happen. No single mechanism catches everything.

### Note on Mongolia

**Critical: The FDD and Franchise Rule are US/Canada/UK instruments.** Mongolia does not have a franchise disclosure statute or franchise law. 

**Implication:** If Pi.mn franchises within Mongolia only, you may not be legally required to file an FDD or franchise agreement—but it is **extremely strongly recommended** for your own protection:
- A written franchise agreement protects both you and the franchisee by defining obligations.
- An operations manual demonstrates you have systematized the business and aren't relying on founder magic.
- You should consult a Mongolian business attorney to understand local corporate law (if any specific rules apply to licensing a method or brand).

**If franchising internationally** (to China, Russia, Southeast Asia, or Western countries), you will need full FDD compliance and state/country-specific legal work for each region. This significantly increases costs.

## 6. THE PILOT REQUIREMENT: Is 4 Company-Owned Branches Enough?

### The Standard Advice

Most franchise consultants say: *"You need 2–5 company-owned locations and ideally 1–2 pilot franchises before selling franchises."*

**Reality check:** This is best practice, not a legal requirement (in the US). Some franchisors have sold franchises with only one company-owned location and succeeded; others have failed. What matters is whether the business is truly **founder-dependent** or **system-dependent**.

### What Your 4 Branches Prove

**You have:**
- Consistent operations across multiple locations (good signal)
- Real unit economics from 4 data points (strong)
- A team of 13 teachers (shows you can hire and train)
- ~359 students with reproducible cohort outcomes (excellent)

**What you don't have:**
- Documented evidence that a non-founder manager can successfully open and run a new location from scratch
- A pilot franchise operated by an independent owner (ideally someone without prior exam-prep experience)

### The Honest Answer

**You do not have a legal obligation to run a pilot franchise before selling, but you have a business risk.**

**Risk scenario:** You sell 3 franchises in 2025 based on your 4 branches' data. If those franchises underperform (because your operations manual was incomplete, or the teaching method depends heavily on subtle teacher judgment), franchisees will demand refunds or sue. Your brand will be damaged in a small market (Mongolia).

**Minimum viable alternative to avoid this:**

Before selling your first franchise, do ONE of the following:

1. **Pilot franchise (recommended):** Open 1–2 franchises with external owners (not family/friends) in a different city. Run them for 12 months. Document what worked and what broke in your operations manual. Cost: $20,000–$50,000 per pilot in setup support + 6–12 months of troubleshooting. Timeline: 12–18 months before you can sell confidently.

2. **Operator pilot (faster):** Hire a non-founder manager (someone without ties to Pi.mn) to open and run a new company-owned location from scratch, using only your operations manual. If they succeed within 6–9 months, you have proof of replicability. Cost: manager salary (~$2,000–$4,000/month) + location setup (~$20,000–$50,000). Timeline: 9–12 months.

3. **Franchisee as beta tester (riskier):** Sell your first franchise with a steep discount (20–30% off franchise fees) and explicit mutual agreement that you'll jointly troubleshoot the operations manual together. Frame it as a "partnership pilot." Cost: reduced franchise fees; risk: you're bound to a contract while debugging your system.

### Recommendation for Pi.mn

**Given your stage and market size:** Option 2 (hire an external operator for a new location) is the fastest path. You already have 4 company-owned branches with experienced staff. Hire a manager (ideally someone with education/tutoring experience but no exam-prep background), give them your operations manual, and let them build a new branch in a different district. This takes 6–9 months and costs ~$30,000–$60,000.

If successful, you have proof the system works without you. Then you can sell franchises confidently.

## 8. EDUCATION-FRANCHISE FAILURE MODES

### Why Education Franchises Fail (Post-Mortems)

**Root cause #1: Value Proposition Collapse**
- Franchisee charges high fees to parents but pays teachers poorly (to hit profit margins franchisor designed for corporate-owned units).
- Parents perceive value as low, enrollment drops. Teacher turnover spikes (underpaid staff leave).
- **Symptom:** High franchisee turnover; locations close within 2–3 years.
- **Prevention:** Design a unit economics model that allows franchisees to pay teachers 20–40% above local market rate AND achieve 20%+ net margin. If you can't do that, your model doesn't work.

**Root cause #2: Market Over-Saturation & Weak Brand**
- Franchisor opens too many locations in one area, cannibalizing each other's enrollments.
- Or: Brand has weak recognition and requires massive local marketing before enrollments materialize.
- **Symptom:** New franchisees spend 6–12 months in "ramp" with very low enrollments; they burn cash and fail.
- **Prevention:** Strict territory controls. Proof that a location can achieve break-even within 6–9 months (using real data from your 4 branches).

**Root cause #3: Founder-Dependent Teaching Quality**
- Franchisees hire teachers but cannot replicate the founder's coaching, pacing, or problem-selection judgment.
- Student outcomes (pass rates, score improvements) diverge wildly across locations.
- Parents in franchised locations get lower quality; word-of-mouth kills enrollments.
- **Symptom:** Franchised locations underperform company-owned locations by >20%.
- **Prevention:** Ruthless documentation of teaching method + mandatory 2–3 week mentoring per teacher + annual refresher training.

**Root cause #4: No Quality Control / Race to the Bottom**
- Franchisor has no audits, no student outcome tracking, no visibility into franchisee operations.
- Franchisees cut corners (hire unqualified teachers, reduce test rigor, overcrowd classes).
- Brand reputation suffers; parents lose trust.
- **Symptom:** Customer complaints spike; some franchisees close due to low demand; others cheat on fees/royalties.
- **Prevention:** Implement the four quality mechanisms (audits, outcome tracking, mystery shoppers, franchisee scorecard) and enforce them consistently. Cost: ~$12,000–$20,000/year per franchisee.

**Root cause #5: Poor Unit Economics**
- Franchisor inflates estimated startup costs and/or underestimates monthly overhead (rent, utilities, insurance).
- Franchisees spend $100,000 to open but take 18+ months to break even instead of 9 months.
- They run out of cash and fail.
- **Prevention:** Use real data from your 4 branches to build a bulletproof unit economics model. Show franchisees exactly what to expect: startup cost range, monthly expenses by line item, enrollment ramp, break-even month, 3-year cumulative cash flow. If you can't show this with confidence, don't franchise yet.

**Root cause #6: Insufficient Franchisee Support**
- Franchisor provides manual + 1-week training and then disappears.
- Franchisee has no ongoing access to expertise when problems arise (student retention drops, a teacher leaves unexpectedly, local competition emerges).
- Franchisee feels abandoned and blames franchisor.
- **Prevention:** Budget $60,000–$120,000/year in salaries for a franchisee support team (even a part-time person). Regular calls, quick problem-solving, marketing advice, teacher recruitment help. This is not optional.

**Root cause #7: Teaching Method Does Not Scale / Is Culturally Dependent**
- The founder's method assumes certain student behavior (e.g., high homework compliance, strong parental support, minimal classroom disruption).
- In a different geographic/demographic context, students behave differently and the method flops.
- **Prevention:** Before franchising, test your method in a location with different student demographics (different socioeconomic status, family background, learning needs). If it works, you have a scalable system. If it doesn't, adapt the method and re-test.

## SUMMARY: 10-LINE EXECUTIVE BRIEF

1. **Pre-launch checklist:** An education franchisor must build a 300–500-page operations manual, documented teaching method (with trainer certification protocols), teacher training system, quality control mechanisms (audits + outcome tracking), legal documents (FDD + franchise agreement), trademark registration, and proof of non-founder transferability. ~7–8 major deliverables, none of which you currently have.

2. **Teaching method transfer:** Your method's procedures (problem selection, testing, marking protocol, cohort adaptation rules) are documented and teachable through 2–3 week mentoring. The tacit judgment (pacing, student-difficulty recognition) cannot be fully codified but is mitigated by structured observation, certification, and annual refresher training. Without this rigor, franchisees' student outcomes will diverge 20%+ from yours.

3. **Company-owned branches suffice as proof, but risky:** Your 4 branches + 13 teachers + 359 students provide strong unit economics data. However, you lack proof that a non-founder manager or franchisee can replicate your success from scratch using only a manual. Run one external operator location for 6–9 months before selling franchises; this costs $30,000–$60,000 and takes 9–12 months but de-risks franchising significantly.

4. **Realistic cost to become franchisable:** $111,000–$427,000. Low end assumes in-house documentation + Mongolia-only legal work. High end includes franchise consultant, technology infrastructure, and multi-country trademark registration. Expect 60–70% of costs in the first 6 months; break-even on franchising investment typically takes 2.5–3 years (assuming 3–5 franchises sold at ~6% royalty).

5. **Timeline:** 6–12 months to first franchise sale (if you skip a formal pilot); 12–18 months if you run a pilot franchise. Phase 1 (months 0–6) is heavy lifting: FDD, operations manual, pilot hiring. Phase 2 (months 6–12) is testing and refinement. Phase 3 (months 12+) is franchisee support and growth.

6. **Quality control is non-negotiable:** Education franchises fail when teaching quality diverges across locations. Budget $12,000–$20,000/year per franchisee in direct franchisor QA (audits, outcome tracking, mystery shoppers, annual in-person training). Without this, franchisees will underperform, parents will notice, and your brand will erode within 2–3 years.

7. **The pilot question answered:** You don't have a legal obligation to run a pilot franchise, but you have a business risk. If student outcomes in your 3rd franchisee are 20% below your company-owned branches, franchisees will fail and sue. Recommendation: hire a non-founder manager to operate a new company-owned location using only your operations manual (9–12 months). If they succeed, you have proof. This is faster and cheaper than a full pilot franchise.

8. **Unit economics must be bulletproof:** Franchisees fail most often because unit economics were wrong—startup costs were higher or revenue ramps slower than promised. Use real data from your 4 branches to build a detailed model showing: startup cost, monthly expenses (by line item), enrollment ramp (month 1–12), break-even month, 3-year cumulative cash flow. If your model shows break-even at 18+ months, your franchising model is broken; you must reduce costs or prove faster revenue ramp before selling.

9. **The legal requirement note (Mongolia-specific):** Mongolia has no franchise disclosure statute. You are not legally required to file an FDD or franchise agreement. However, you should still prepare a written franchise agreement and operations manual for your own protection. Consult a Mongolian business attorney to understand local corporate law. If you expand internationally, FDD and state-specific legal work will add $15,000–$50,000+ per country.

10. **Education franchise failure mode to avoid:** The most common failure is value-proposition collapse: franchisor designs unit economics assuming $X monthly revenue, but new franchisees in different locations cannot reach $X because of weak brand recognition, intense local competition, or high local rent. Teachers must be paid less to hit profit margins, which causes staff turnover and low student outcomes. Location closes within 2–3 years. Prevention: prove with your 4 branches (or an external operator location) that a new location can achieve break-even within 6–9 months even with competitive local salaries.
