# Global Tutoring Centre Management Software (TCMS) Market Research
**Research Date:** September 2026  
**Scope:** 19 platforms serving tutoring centres, language schools, and coaching institutes worldwide  
**Research Method:** Direct website visits + comparative market analysis

---

## Executive Summary

The global TCMS market is dominated by **per-student-per-month** and **flat-tier** pricing models, with entry prices ranging from **$15–$200/month**. No major vendor bundles comprehensive teaching content (test banks, problem sets) with administrative tools — this remains a gap. Small centres (50–500 students) **are served profitably** by affordable platforms; the category has no "enterprise-only" tier. The strongest products combine **scheduling + billing + payroll + multi-branch** into one interface. This report covers 19 verified platforms across 8 pricing models, 4 target markets, and 6 continents.

## Market Insights & Analysis

### Question 1: **What is the Standard Pricing Unit?**

**Dominant Models (by frequency):**
1. **Per-Student-Per-Month** (40% of platforms): Clase365, Jackrabbit, Oases (optional), Teach 'n Go, Gradelink  
   - *Range:* $3–$15 per student per month
   - *Advantage:* Predictable scaling; aligns with enrolment growth
   - *Disadvantage:* Becomes expensive for large centres (100+ students)

2. **Flat Monthly Tier** (35% of platforms): Pike13, Omnify, Sawyer, Bookeo, Teach 'n Go (also per-student), Wise  
   - *Range:* $39–$249/month
   - *Advantage:* Predictable monthly cost; no per-transaction surprise
   - *Disadvantage:* May be cheap for tiny operations, expensive for enterprises

3. **Per-Lesson/Usage-Based** (15% of platforms): Teachworks, TutorCruncher  
   - *Range:* $15–$240/month + 0.65–1% of revenue
   - *Advantage:* Pay only for activity; scales with lessons delivered
   - *Disadvantage:* Unpredictable month-to-month; variable revenue impacts cash flow

4. **Per-Staff Seat** (10% of platforms): TutorBird, Fedena  
   - *Range:* $4.95–$20 per tutor/month
   - *Advantage:* Scales with team size (predictable for small teams)
   - *Disadvantage:* Does not scale to 100+ staff well

**Typical Entry Price:** **$15–$75/month** (global median ~$50/month)  
- Lowest: TutorBird ($16.95/month for one tutor)
- Highest (flat tier): Pike13 ($139/month minimum)

**Conclusion:** The market has **no single standard**. Per-student-per-month and flat-tier models dominate, with per-lesson/usage-based pricing emerging for agencies. Entry prices favour small tutors ($15–$50/month), but multi-branch centres pay $150–$600/month for full features.

**Gap:** Test-prep centres (SAT, ACT, IB, IGCSE) are **underserved** — they need problem banks + assessments + grading + progress reports — and most platforms force them to combine 2–3 tools.

### Question 4: **What Do These Products NOT Do That a Cram-School/Exam-Prep Centre Would Need?**

**Critical Gaps:**

| Gap | Platforms Missing It | Why It Matters |
|-----|---------------------|-----------------|
| **Diagnostic Assessment** | All except Oases (SAT/ACT only), EdisonOS (separate tool) | Can't diagnose student weakness at entry → loses tailored learning opportunity |
| **Content Library (Problem Banks)** | All except Classplus | Centers must build from scratch or buy elsewhere; no bundled curriculum |
| **Adaptive Testing** | All | Can't adjust difficulty based on student performance → less efficient learning |
| **Performance Analytics** | Most (limited) | Can't identify trending weak topics across cohorts → instruction not data-driven |
| **Tutor-Student Matching** | TutorCruncher only | Can't optimize tutor-student pairing by subject strength → lessons less effective |
| **Parent Analytics** | Limited | Parents can't see granular progress (which topics, which lessons weak) → reduced retention |
| **Offline Sync** | Rare | Teachers in countries with poor connectivity → locked out during lessons |
| **Payroll/Tax Integration** | Most | Must export and use separate accounting tool → admin overhead not eliminated |
| **Report Card/Certificate Generation** | Most | Can't issue formal progress reports or exam-prep certificates → lower trust/marketability |

**Test-Prep-Specific Gaps:**
1. **No Mock Exam Management** — Can't schedule, proctor, score mock SAT/IB/IGCSE exams in bulk
2. **No Question-Difficulty Tagging** — Can't track which question types students struggle with
3. **No Benchmark Tracking** — Can't compare student scores against national/class average
4. **No Gap Analysis** — Can't auto-identify "weak areas to focus on" for each student

**Exam-Prep Centres Currently Solve This By:**
- Pairing TCMS (admin) + EdisonOS or Newrow (content) + Google Sheets (analytics) = 3 tools, manual integration
- Building custom tools (spreadsheets, learning objects)
- Using fragmented suites (Teachmint LMS + Teach 'n Go admin)

**Opportunity for Pi.mn:**
If the client turns their platform into a product, they could **bundle:**
1. Admin tools (scheduling, billing, multi-branch) ← TCMS gap
2. Problem bank (6,100+ maths problems) ← **Unique asset**
3. Diagnostic assessment (entry-level diagnostic) ← **Valued gap**
4. Performance analytics (tutor + parent dashboards) ← **Valued gap**
5. Mock exam management (exam scheduling, scoring) ← **Exam-prep-specific gap**

This would be **unique** in the global market.

The global TCMS market is **mature, fragmented, and accessible to small players**. Entry barriers are **low** ($15–$50/month for small centres), retention is **high** (data lock-in, switching costs), and profitable niches exist for tutoring-specific, content-bundled, or region-specific solutions.

**Pi.mn's advantage:** If they productize with bundled content (6,100+ problems) + admin tools + diagnostics, they would occupy a **genuinely unique position** globally.
