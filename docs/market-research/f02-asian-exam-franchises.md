## Detailed Chain Profiles

### **INDIA: Aakash Institute (AESL)**

**Franchise Model:** Hybrid (70 franchised + 130+ company-owned across 200+ centres)

**Investment & Royalty:**
- Franchise fee: ₹6–18 lakh (US$7,200–21,600) depending on city tier and centre type
- **Ongoing royalty: 20% of gross revenue** (covers brand, support, digital platform, faculty training)
- Total investment: ₹28–125 lakh (US$33,600–150,000)
- **Break-even timeline: 10–24 months** for efficiently managed centres in education-focused areas

**What Franchisees Receive:**
1. **Curriculum & Content:** Centralised, updated academic materials for NEET/JEE Main/JEE Advanced/Foundation courses
2. **AIATS (All India Aakash Test Series):** Mock test system with national ranking; ~6,100+ problems; free for enrolled students; core product that binds network
3. **Faculty Training:** Systematic recruitment, training, and certification programs; continuous pedagogical updates
4. **Operations Support:** Detailed operations manuals, intensive training in test-prep business, technology infrastructure, monthly performance reports
5. **Marketing & Brand:** Centralised advertising, corporate campaigns, brand leverage
6. **Centre Visits:** Regular monitoring by experienced faculty and administrative staff

**Quality Control Mechanisms:**
- Faculty vetting and systematic training programmes
- Monthly performance reporting and monitoring
- Centre visits by central team
- Emphasis on student outcomes (JEE/NEET success rate)
- Renewal conditions every 3–5 years based on performance

**Mock Exam System as Binding Product:**
The **AIATS (All India Aakash Test Series)** is the franchise network's binding asset. Students compete nationally, receive percentile scores, estimated All India Rank (AIR), and can benchmark against peers across all 200 Aakash centres. This creates:
- Network effects: franchisees cannot opt out without losing competitive advantage
- Student retention: parents expect national benchmarking, not local tests
- Franchisee differentiation: quality teaching attracts students into AIATS pipeline

**Current Status (2025–26):**
- Successfully separated from BYJU'S after Supreme Court ruling (November 2025); diluted BYJU'S stake to <5% through rights issue (February 2026)
- Independent operations with strong franchise network
- Profitable model; serving ~1 million students
- Expansion into hybrid learning centres (online + offline)

**Revenue Share Example:** Franchisee with 100 students at ₹50,000/year per student = ₹50 lakh gross; 20% royalty = ₹10 lakh to Aakash; franchisee keeps ₹40 lakh (before salaries, rent, other costs)

### **INDIA: ALLEN Career Institute**

**Franchise Model:** **PRIMARILY COMPANY-OWNED** (NOT a franchised chain despite industry competition)

**Investment:**
- Minimum ₹3 crore (US$360,000); often ₹15+ crore per centre
- **ALLEN DOES NOT offer regular small franchises**; only strategic partnerships with large, well-funded operators

**Why Company-Owned Model?**
- Tight control over teaching quality (critical in high-stakes exams)
- Centralised curriculum delivery and faculty management
- Integrated with Kota headquarters operations

**What Company-Owned Centres Receive:**
- Centralised curriculum, doubt-resolution systems, structured study materials
- Question banks and exam simulations
- Faculty hired, trained, and managed centrally
- Large-scale classroom infrastructure

**Quality Control:**
- Direct oversight via company hierarchy
- Regular performance monitoring
- Teaching standards enforcement
- Student outcome tracking

**Mock Exam Role:**
All-India mock tests; students ranked nationally. Central to product offering.

**Current Status & Challenges (2025–26):**
- **Expansion struggling:** 100+ new centres opened in Tier 2/3 cities post-$600M Bodhi Tree investment (2022)
- **Profitability crisis:** Majority of new centres losing money; many unviable
- **FY25 profit decline:** Company-owned expansion model financially stressed
- **Kota dominance shifting:** 75% of revenue now from outside Kota (vs. historical reliance on Kota)
- Model under re-evaluation after growth failures outside Kota

**Key Insight:** Company ownership provides quality control but sacrifices scalability; ALLEN's expansion failure vs. franchised competitors (Aakash, FIITJEE) suggests **exam-prep franchise market rewards franchising over company control** when scaling nationally.

### **CHINA: New Oriental & TAL Education (Xueersi)**

**PRE-2021 Model (Gaokao Exam Prep):**

Both were China's largest tutoring chains, franchised nationally, preparing students for the **gaokao** (高考, college entrance exam).

**Business Model (Pre-2021):**
- Franchised network of 1000+ centres
- Online lectures + offline classrooms
- Question banks, teaching materials, teacher training
- Chain-wide mock exams with national ranking
- Royalty-based franchise agreements

**2021 China Crackdown — Complete Industry Collapse:**

In July 2021, China's State Council banned:
- All K–12 tutoring (weekend/holiday/after-school)
- Establishment of new tutoring centres
- Tutoring for profit (converted to non-profit if operating)

**Impact:**
- **New Oriental:** 60,000 staff laid off; $3.13B severance, lease termination, tuition refunds paid; stock collapsed 70%; CEO lost billionaire status
- **TAL Education:** Stock collapsed 80%; earnings calls halted
- **Industry:** Essentially eliminated overnight

**Post-2021 Pivot:**
- New Oriental: Adults-only EdTech (English, coding), publishing, writing classes, tabletop gaming
- TAL: Similar pivot to non-exam-prep services
- **Exam-prep franchise business:** Non-existent; effectively destroyed

**Lessons for Mongolia:**
- Regulatory risk is **existential** in exam-prep franchising
- Government can eliminate entire industries overnight (as in China)
- Mongolia's ЭЕШ franchise model should anticipate policy changes (even if not imminent)

## Four Key Questions: Findings & Analysis

### **1. Is Franchising or Company-Ownership the Dominant Expansion Model in Exam Prep, and Why?**

**Finding: Franchising dominates for rapid scaling; company-ownership dominates for quality control.**

| Chain | Model | Rationale |
|-------|-------|-----------|
| Aakash (India) | **Hybrid (70 franchise, 130 company-owned)** | Franchise for speed; company-owned for flagship/control markets |
| FIITJEE (India) | **Franchised** | Pure franchising; rapid national expansion |
| ALLEN (India) | **Company-owned only** | Quality control prioritised; scalability sacrificed (expansion struggling) |
| Narayana, Sri Chaitanya (India) | **Franchised** | Large networks; proven franchise model works at scale |
| Megastudy (Korea) | **Vertically integrated (not franchised)** | Vertical integration (lectures + books + exams) at scale replaces franchising |
| Daekyo (Korea) | **Conglomerate (not franchised)** | Multi-brand corporate structure |
| SAPIX (Japan) | **Company-owned (selective admission)** | Quality via student selection, not franchisee standardisation |
| Meikō, Tokyo Individualized (Japan) | **Franchised** | Japanese juku sector most franchised in Asia |
| New Oriental, TAL (China) | **Franchised (pre-2021); destroyed 2021** | Franchised until regulatory destruction |

**Conclusion:**
- **Franchising is faster for national expansion** (Aakash, FIITJEE, Narayana, Sri Chaitanya, Japanese juku)
- **Company-ownership provides tighter quality control** but limits scale (ALLEN's model failed at expansion; SAPIX chose selectivity over scale)
- **Vertically integrated conglomerates** (Korea's Megastudy, Daekyo) operate at large scale by internalising content, exams, and distribution
- **Optimal model:** Hybrid franchising (Aakash) or vertical integration (Korea) for exam-prep at national scale
- **Pure company-ownership struggles to scale** (ALLEN's experience)

**For Mongolia:**
Franchising appears **necessary for ЭЕШ tutoring centre expansion beyond current 4 centres**. Company-ownership alone will limit growth; vertical integration (building test engine + curriculum + franchisee support) provides competitive moat.

### **2. Is the Chain-Wide Standardised Mock Exam the Core Binding Product in Exam-Prep Franchising?**

**Finding: YES. Chain-wide mock exams are the franchise's binding asset.**

**Evidence:**

| Chain | Mock Exam System | Binding Function |
|-------|------------------|-----------------|
| Aakash | AIATS (All India Aakash Test Series) | National ranking; 6,100+ problems; free for enrolled students; prevents franchisee defection (students expect network benchmarking) |
| FIITJEE | National test series | JEE/NEET ranking; franchisees cannot opt out without losing competitive advantage |
| Sri Chaitanya | AITS (All India Test Series) | National ranking; binds network; students compete across all 850+ centres |
| Korean hagwons | Chain-wide CSAT mocks | National CSAT benchmarking; student retention via competitive ranking |
| SAPIX | Extensive integrated mocks | Tracking students across cohorts; entrance exam preparation; **non-franchised SAPIX achieves same binding function via selectivity** |
| Meikō, Japanese juku | Entrance exam mocks | Students ranked within network |
| New Oriental, TAL (pre-2021) | Gaokao mocks with national ranking | Franchisees bound by student expectation of national benchmarking |

**Why Binding?**
1. **Student Retention:** Parents pay premium for entrance-exam coaching expecting **national benchmarking** (i.e., "How does my child rank against 100,000 JEE aspirants?"), not just local comparison
2. **Franchisee Differentiation:** A franchisee cannot withdraw from the mock exam network without losing student competitive advantage
3. **Quality Assurance:** Chain-wide standardised tests serve as **proxy for franchisee quality** (low-performing franchises show in rankings)
4. **Network Effects:** Every franchisee's reputation tied to system-wide performance; incentivises quality

**Comparison to Kumon (Non-Exam Franchising):**
- Kumon (general math/reading skill development) does NOT rely on chain-wide mocks
- Kumon franchise model works via **standardised workbooks + self-paced learning**, not exams
- Exam-prep franchising **requires** mock system; skill-development franchising does not

**Critical Insight:**
The client's **6,100-problem bank + AIATS-equivalent mock engine across 4 centres** is their franchise asset. This is **not peripheral**; it is the **binding product that enables franchisee expansion**. Without it, franchisees would lack differentiation.

**For Mongolia:**
The ЭЕШ-specific problem bank + standardised mock exam system is **the core franchise asset**. Franchisee expansion is viable because:
1. Franchisees inherit national test system (don't have to build it)
2. Students enroll expecting ЭЕШ-specific preparation with national benchmarking
3. Mock exam performance serves as quality assurance proxy
4. Network effects lock in franchisees and students

### **3. How Do Exam-Prep Franchises Control Quality Across Distributed Networks to Prevent Franchisee Brand Damage?**

**Finding: Multi-layered quality control; no single mechanism sufficient. Mock exam performance serves as primary control lever.**

| Control Mechanism | How It Works | Examples |
|-------------------|--------------|----------|
| **Faculty Training & Certification** | Centralised training programs; recurring updates; franchisees cannot hire independently | Aakash: "systematic recruitment and training programs"; FIITJEE: "partner excellence in teaching standards" |
| **Monthly/Quarterly Performance Reporting** | Franchisees submit student results, test scores, attendance data; centre visits by central team | Aakash: "monthly performance reports"; "centre visits by experienced faculty" |
| **Standardised Curriculum & Materials** | All franchisees use identical lesson plans, question banks, explanations; reduces variance | FIITJEE: centralised curriculum; Aakash: academic updates/pedagogy support |
| **Mock Exam Performance as Proxy** | National ranking reveals weak franchisees; low-performing centres visible to students and parents | AIATS/AITS rankings; students can see which centres' students perform poorly nationally |
| **Renewal Conditions & Performance-Based Contracts** | Franchisee renewed only if performance targets met (e.g., X% JEE/NEET pass rate) | Aakash: renewal every 3–5 years based on performance; FIITJEE: result-based accountability |
| **Royalty-Based Alignment** | 20% royalty on gross revenue incentivises franchisee to operate well (higher revenue = higher royalty) | Aakash 20% royalty; FIITJEE estimated 15–20% |
| **Unannounced Audits & Surprise Visits** | Central team visits centres unannounced; checks teaching standards, facility, conduct | Common practice; mentioned by Aakash |
| **Student Feedback & Reviews** | Public rankings, student testimonials, online reviews; poor franchisees suffer enrolment loss | Increasingly common; students/parents compare centres via rankings |
| **Franchisee Selection (Vetting)** | Rigorous initial partner selection; FIITJEE emphasises "ownership, accountability, proactive leadership" | FIITJEE: strict partner vetting; Aakash: requires demonstrated business acumen |
| **Technology/Software Control** | Centralised learning management system, student tracking, progress monitoring; franchisees cannot diverge | Aakash: "technology and legal support"; digital platform access |

**Weaknesses & Limits:**
- **Mock exams reveal average quality, not individual franchisee variance:** A weak teaching franchisee might inflate scores via easy tests; centralized mock exams only catch this if national average diverges
- **Teacher attrition:** Even with training, centres in Tier 2/3 cities struggle to retain quality faculty (salary, career growth)
- **Franchisee owner commitment:** A financially-stressed franchisee may cut corners on teacher salaries or facility upkeep; hard to detect without unannounced audits
- **Cultural variance:** Quality norms in Kota (ALLEN's city) differ from Tier 3 cities; standardisation imperfect

**Why Quality Control in Exam-Prep is Harder Than Kumon:**
- Kumon: standardised workbooks, self-paced, mechanical scoring → easy to standardise
- Exam-prep: teacher quality, curriculum depth, doubt-resolution speed, mock exam rigour → harder to standardise; depends on teaching talent and franchisee commitment

**For Mongolia:**
The ЭЕШ franchise should implement:
1. Centralised ЭЕШ curriculum (non-negotiable)

## Summary & Synthesis: The Franchise Model for High-Stakes Exam Prep

### **How Exam-Prep Franchising Differs from Other Education Franchising (e.g., Kumon, general tutoring):**

1. **Mock exam system is non-negotiable:** All major chains (India, Korea, Japan) operate chain-wide standardised tests with national/regional ranking. This is **not a feature**; it is the **core product**. Kumon does not require this.

2. **Teaching quality is more critical:** Exam-prep success depends on **teaching excellence + curriculum depth + doubt resolution speed**. Kumon's model (self-paced workbooks) is less teacher-dependent. Exam-prep franchises must control for teacher quality more rigorously.

3. **Regulatory risk is existential:** China's 2021 crackdown eliminated the entire exam-prep franchise industry overnight. No other education franchise faces this risk to the same degree (generic tutoring, skill-based learning, language prep are less politically sensitive).

4. **Franchising vs. vertical integration trade-off:** 
   - **India:** Franchising dominates (Aakash, FIITJEE, Narayana, Sri Chaitanya) — rapid scaling, distributed network
   - **Korea:** Vertical integration dominates (Megastudy, Daekyo) — conglomerate control, in-house exams + content + distribution
   - **Japan:** Franchising common (Meikō, Tokyo Individualized) but non-franchised SAPIX (via selectivity) also succeeds
   - **Lesson:** At scale (national footprint), successful chains converge to **either vertical integration OR pure franchising with rigorous quality control**; hybrid company-owned/franchised (Aakash) is viable intermediate.

5. **Franchisee economics are tight:** Gross margins 40–62%; after 20% royalty, net margins 20–42%. Break-even 10–24 months. This requires:
   - High student capacity utilisation (minimise empty seats)
   - Minimal teacher attrition (turnover = retraining cost)
   - Low facility costs (Tier 1 city rents are killers)
   - Local reputation (word-of-mouth, alumni networks)

## Transferable Model for Mongolia (ЭЕШ Franchise)

Based on research of successful exam-prep chains, **the most transferable model for Mongolia is a Hybrid-Franchised architecture similar to Aakash:**

**Recommended Structure:**

| Component | Model | Rationale |
|-----------|-------|-----------|
| **Ownership:** | 40% company-owned flagship centres + 60% franchised partners | Flagship centres in Ulaanbaatar for quality control & brand; franchised in Tier 2 cities (Darkhan, Govi-Altai, etc.) |
| **Franchise Fee:** | ₮ 30–80M (US$10,500–28,000) depending on city tier | Competitive vs. Indian chains; reflects Mongolia's lower market capitalization |
| **Ongoing Royalty:** | **18–22% of gross revenue** | Covers ЭЕШ curriculum, mock exams, faculty training, tech support, brand marketing |
| **What Franchisees Get:** | Centralised ЭЕШ curriculum, mock exam system (6,100+ problems), faculty training, operations manual, tech platform, brand usage | Identical to Aakash/FIITJEE model; reduces franchisee development burden |
| **Quality Control:** | Monthly reporting, unannounced audits, teacher certification, mock exam performance monitoring, renewal tied to ЭЕШ pass rate | Multi-layered; mock exam performance is primary lever (public ranking reveals poor centres) |
| **Mock Exam System:** | Centralised, secured, national ranking for all franchisees; runs quarterly (4 mock exams/year aligned to ЭЕШ calendar) | **Core binding asset**; prevents franchisee defection; student retention via competitive ranking |
| **Faculty Support:** | Centralised training program (2–4 days/quarter); certification in ЭЕШ curriculum; salary guidance (to prevent brain drain) | Keeps teaching quality high; mitigates Tier 2 city turnover risk |
| **Technology:** | Learning management system (LMS) for progress tracking; mock exam platform with secure proctoring; student portal; analytics dashboard | Reduces franchisee tech burden; enables central monitoring |
| **Marketing:** | Centralised brand campaigns + local franchisee marketing; Aakash-style national ad strategy | Leverage brand at national level; franchisees invest locally |
| **Profitability Target:** | Franchisee break-even: 12–18 months (faster than India, due to smaller market); net margin target: 25–35% after royalty | Achievable in Ulaanbaatar/Tier 1; Tier 2 cities may require ₮50M investment to reach break-even |

**Why This Model Works for Mongolia:**

1. **Scalability:** Franchising enables expansion to Darkhan, Govi-Altai, Arkhangai without parent capital burden
2. **Quality control:** Centralised mock exams + curriculum prevent divergence; poor franchisees revealed by rankings
3. **Franchisee profitability:** 18–22% royalty allows 25–35% net margin (after salaries, rent); attractive to partners
4. **Regulatory compliance:** Centralised curriculum ensures alignment with ЭЕШ standards; easier to defend vs. regulators
5. **Network effects:** Chain-wide mock exams create lock-in (franchisees cannot exit; students expect national benchmarking)
6. **Risk mitigation:** If China-style regulation hits, franchisees absorb cost (they are separate legal entities); parent is protected
