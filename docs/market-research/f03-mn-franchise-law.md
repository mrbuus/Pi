## 1. Legal Framework for Franchising in Mongolia

### Statutory Foundation

Mongolia does **not** have a dedicated franchise law. Instead, franchising is regulated through general commercial and investment legislation:

- **Civil Code, Chapter 29 (Articles 333–338)** — Governs franchise contracts as licensing of intellectual property (company names, trademarks, product designs, know-how)
- **Foreign Investment Law, Article 5.1.5** — Authorizes franchising on equal terms with other forms of investment
- **Law on Permits** — Governs sector-specific licensing

**Source:** [Mongolia - Franchising | export.gov](https://legacy.export.gov/article?id=Mongolia-Franchising); [Franchising Proves Effective for Many Foreign Brands in Mongolia | Mongolian Law Blog](http://lehmanlaw.mn/blog/franchising-proves-effective-for-many-foreign-brands-in-mongolia/)

### What the Civil Code Requires

Article 333 stipulates that exploitation of intellectual property (including trademarks and names) **must** be done under a written contract specifying:
- Items and subject matter of the franchise
- Price and payment terms
- Content of the agreement
- Liability for contract breach

However, the law does **not** mandate:
- Pre-sale disclosure documents (like U.S. Franchise Disclosure Documents)
- Franchisor registration with a government agency
- Specific relationship terms (territorial rights, renewal, termination)
- Financial or litigation history disclosure

**Critical Finding:** There is no case law or regulatory guidance on franchise disputes in Mongolia. The legal framework is **untested for education franchises**.

## 2. Trademark and Intellectual Property

### Trademark Registration: Process and Cost

**Governing Body:** Intellectual Property Office of Mongolia (IPOM)  
**Address:** Peace Avenue 30, 3rd Khoroo, Bayanzurkh District, Ulaanbaatar 13381

#### Timeline

- **Standard Procedure:** 9–12 months (with possibility of 6-month extension if objections arise)
- **Accelerated Procedure:** 6 months (if expedited examination is requested)
- **Opposition Period:** 3 months from publication in Official Intellectual Property Bulletin

#### Cost (USD)

| Item | Cost |
|------|------|
| Filing fee (first class of goods/services) | $200–400 |
| Additional class | $300 per class |
| Items beyond 10 per class | $25 per item |
| Service provider handling (e.g., via iPNOTE) | $342+ (all-in) |

#### Protection and Renewal

- Valid for **10 years** from registration date
- Renewable indefinitely in 10-year periods upon payment of renewal fees
- Exclusive rights include: use, licensing, sale/transfer, preventing infringement, preventing dilution, and claiming damages

**Sources:** [Trademark registration in Mongolia | Grata Net](https://gratanet.com/publications/trademark-registration-in-mongolia); [MongoDB Trademark Registration Costs | ip-coster.com](https://ip-coster.com/IPGuides/trademark-mongolia)

### Can a Teaching Method Be Protected?

**No.** Teaching methodologies are **not patentable** and cannot be registered as trademark (which protect brands, not processes). However:

- **Trademark on the brand name** (e.g., "Pi.mn", "Challenge Centre") — fully protectable; gives legal rights to sue for counterfeit use
- **Copyright on written materials** (problem books, student manuals, teacher guides) — automatically protected under Law on Copyright and Related Rights (2006); does not require registration
- **Trade Secrets** (curriculum details, proprietary content sequencing) — protected by contract and general unfair-competition law, but not enforced reliably (see Section 4 below)

### Copyright Protection and Enforcement

**Governing Law:** Law on Mongolia on Copyright and Related Rights (1993, revised 2006)  
**International Status:** Mongolia joined WIPO in 2002 and is party to four international copyright conventions

**What's Protected:** Scientific and literary works, computer programs, musical works, fine art, photographs, audiovisual works

**Enforcement Mechanisms:**
- Criminal sanctions (under Criminal Law and Law on Offence)
- Civil remedies (damages claims under Civil Code)
- Administrative penalties

**Major Caveat:** Enforcement is **slow and unpredictable**. Educational material piracy is widespread in Mongolia (documented in earlier research on educational publishing). Courts take months or years to adjudicate IP claims. **No published case law** shows successful enforcement of copyright against tutoring-centre material piracy.

**Sources:** [Copyright in Mongolia | Grata Net](https://gratanet.com/publications/copyright-in-mongolia); [Law on Copyright and Related Rights | WIPO](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/traditional-knowledge/w/tklaws/article_0130)

## 3. Education Franchises Currently Operating in Mongolia

### International Franchises

#### Kumon

**Status:** Pilot phase only (as of February 2025)

- Kumon Institute of Education and Mongolia's Ministry of Education signed a **Memorandum of Understanding** in February 2025 to implement a "Pilot Project for Introducing the Kumon Method in Mongolia"
- **No physical Kumon Learning Centers** are currently operating in Mongolia
- This is Mongolia's first formal introduction to the Kumon franchise model

**Source:** [Ministry of Education of Mongolia and the Kumon Institute Sign MOU | MONTSAME](https://montsame.mn/en/read/361771)

#### Other International Education Franchises

Search for Helen Doron, Eye Level, Wall Street English, Berlitz, and other education franchise brands returned **no evidence of operating centres in Mongolia**.

### Mongolian Domestic Education Chains

#### Пи Тоо (Pi Too) Mathematics Tutoring Center

- **Branches:** 2 locations
- **Staff:** 16 teachers total
- **Offerings:** Mathematics instruction focused on catch-up, advancement, and entrance-exam preparation for international schools; taught in English and Mongolian
- **Model:** Company-owned branches (not franchised)

**Source:** [Vipexpo.mn | Пи тоо Математик Сургалтын төв](http://vipexpo.mn/uyo/bsq/ebs/299-pi-too-matematik-surgaltyn-tv.html)

#### Other Private Tutoring Sector

- **Wall Street English Mongolia** operates language-focused centres in Ulaanbaatar
- Multiple private English-language schools exist but operate as single entities or company-owned chains, not franchises
- **No evidence of education-sector franchises originating in Mongolia or expanding via franchise model**

**Source:** [Wall Street English Mongolia](https://www.wallstreetenglish.mn/)

### Comparative: Non-Education Domestic Franchises

To understand how franchising works locally, examples of successful Mongolian domestic franchises include:
- **DAC** (automotive retail): 17 franchise branches across Mongolia and 1 in Almaty
- **Technozone LLC** (electronics retail): 10 branded stores nationwide
- **Khan Bank**: 528 branches and 335 ATMs (2024)

These demonstrate that **franchising is viable in Mongolia for retail and services**, but **no locally-originated education franchise has scaled successfully**.

**Source:** [Franchising Proves Effective | Mongolian Law Blog](http://lehmanlaw.mn/blog/franchising-proves-effective-for-many-foreign-brands-in-mongolia/)

## 4. Licensing Requirements for Tutoring/Training Centres in Mongolia

### Regulatory Framework

Private training and tutoring centres are subject to **special permit requirements** under:
- General Law on Education (2023)
- Law on Professional and Technical Education and Training
- Law on Permits for Special and General Licenses
- Ministry of Labor and Social Protection decree (A/104, dated 4 May 2018)

### Required Special Permit

A **special permit (тусгай зөвшөөрөл) for training activities** must be obtained from the competent education authority (Ministry of Education and Science or provincial Education Department) before operation.

### Eligibility and Requirements

#### Personnel (80% Rule)
- **At minimum, 80% of teaching staff must be professional instructors** with documented:
  - Professional credentials and teaching qualifications
  - Three years of relevant work experience
  - Completed pedagogical training
  - Valid teaching authorization certificate

#### Facility Standards
- **Ownership or documented control** of the training venue
- Compliance with **MNS 6541:2015** (Mongolian professional education standards)
- Building layout and architectural documentation
- Safety and hygiene compliance

#### Financial Requirements
- Bank references confirming financial capacity
- Tax clearance and good standing
- **Three-year budget projection** showing:
  - Projected student enrollment growth
  - Instructor-to-student ratios
  - Fee structure and sustainability

#### Curriculum and Academic Requirements
- Approved curriculum (must be reviewed by regional methodological centres)
- For new specialties or military/police training: approval from relevant state administrative agency required

### Application Timeline

- Submit application **at least 6 months** before intended start date
- Application package includes templates 1–14 (specified by decree A/104)
- All documents must be notarized where required

### Permit Renewal and Revocation

- Permit is subject to renewal
- Revocation grounds include:
  - Violation of professional standards
  - Repeated failure to meet permit conditions
  - Negative impact on public welfare or health
  - Failure to begin operations within 6 months of registration
  - Structural changes (mergers, divisions)

## 5. Provincial Demand and Market Opportunity

### Secondary Education Geography

- **Total secondary schools in Mongolia:** 885 (as of latest data)
- **Located outside Ulaanbaatar:** 571 schools (64%)
- **Structure:** Sum centres typically have schools only to grade 8; students seeking grades 9–11 must travel to aimag (province) centres

### Private Tutoring Market

**Documented Demand:**
- Mongolians place high cultural value on education
- Private tutoring has been a growing phenomenon since 1990, with strong demand for exam preparation
- **Language tutoring market** is expanding, especially for English and international school entrance prep
- Monthly teacher salaries in private language centres range ₮1.7–5.36 million ($500–1,500 USD)

**Market Characteristics:**
- Tutoring is often private (parents and tutors may keep it confidential due to perceived stigma regarding school quality)
- Few published market studies exist on the scale of the private tutoring sector
- English proficiency among students is growing, driving demand for language tutoring centres

**Provincial Context:**
- Aimag (provincial) centres are underserved by specialized test-prep and tutoring services
- Students in remote areas face limited access to tutoring, slow information updates, and internet connectivity challenges
- Unmet demand exists for exam-preparation services outside Ulaanbaatar, particularly for secondary graduation exams

## 6. Franchise Dispute Resolution and Enforcement

### Court System for Franchise Disputes

Franchise disputes are adjudicated in civil courts:
- **District Courts** (first instance)
- **City Court of Ulaanbaatar** (appellate)
- **Supreme Court of Mongolia** (final appeal)

### Major Enforcement Challenges

**Judgment Delays:**
- Judges frequently avoid controversial business decisions, preferring to delay judgment indefinitely
- Cases commonly take **years to resolve**—some exceed **four years**
- No expedited commercial court or arbitration preference in practice

**Enforcement Difficulties:**
- Even after judgment, enforcement of court orders faces **significant delays**
- Counterparties frequently **liquidate assets and disappear** before enforcement is executed
- Weak remedies for breached franchise agreements

**Recommended Alternative:**
- Legal experts advise **non-judicial dispute resolution** (negotiation, mediation) for business disputes
- Arbitration clauses in franchise agreements are recommended but require specific legal drafting under Mongolian law

## Summary: Key Feasibility Findings

### 1. Is Franchising Legally Recognized in Mongolia?

**Yes, but minimally regulated.** Mongolia's Civil Code (Articles 333–338) permits franchise agreements. However:
- No dedicated franchise law exists
- No franchisor disclosure or registration requirement
- No regulatory oversight of franchise sales
- **Untested legal framework for education franchises specifically**

### 2. How Are Trademarks Registered and at What Cost?

- **Timeline:** 9–12 months (standard); 6 months (accelerated)
- **Cost per class:** $200–400
- **Additional classes:** $300 each
- **Validity:** 10 years, renewable indefinitely
- **Process:** Apply to IPOM; examine for formal compliance and conflicts; publish for opposition (3-month window); issue certificate
- **Teaching methods cannot be protected**; only brand names, written materials (copyright), and trade secrets (contract-based)

### 3. Are Any Education Franchises Operating in Mongolia?

- **Kumon:** Signed pilot MOU in February 2025; no operating centres yet
- **Other international franchises:** No evidence of Helen Doron, Eye Level, Wall Street English, or Berlitz operating as franchises in Mongolia
- **Mongolian education chains:** None identified using franchise model; domestic tutoring centres operate as company-owned branches only
- **Conclusion:** Education franchising is **practically untested in Mongolia**

### 4. What Licence Does a Tutoring Centre Need?

- **Special Permit (тусгай зөвшөөрөл)** from Ministry of Education and Science or provincial Education Department
- **Key requirements:**
  - 80% of teachers must be professional instructors (3 years experience + credentials)
  - Comply with MNS 6541:2015 standards
  - Own or control facility; produce building documentation
  - Financial capacity + 3-year budget projection
  - Approved curriculum
- **Timeline to obtain:** Submit 6 months before intended start
- **Renewal/revocation:** Subject to ongoing compliance; revocable if standards violated or operations don't begin within 6 months

### 5. What Is the Biggest Legal Risk?

**Franchise Dispute Enforcement and IP Protection Uncertainty**

1. **No franchise-specific enforcement mechanism** — Disputes go to civil courts; delays routinely exceed 1–4 years; counterparties may disappear before enforcement
2. **Copyright enforcement is unpredictable** — Educational material piracy is documented and widespread; no published cases of successful enforcement against tutoring-centre materials
3. **Teaching methods cannot be protected** — Franchisee could copy curriculum and rebrand without legal recourse (only trademark and copyright on materials are defensible)
4. **No franchisor registration or pre-franchise disclosure law** — Franchisee has minimal legal transparency into franchisor's finances, litigation history, or dispute record
5. **Territorial rights unaddressed in law** — Franchise agreement must specify territory; absent that, franchisees may expand into each other's areas with limited contractual remedy

---

## Recommendations for Further Action

1. **Secure trademark and copyright immediately** — Register "Pi.mn" (or brand name) with IPOM; register educational materials with IPOM/copyright office as backup
2. **Engage Mongolian legal counsel** — Draft a franchise agreement with clear:
   - Territory and exclusivity terms
   - Termination and renewal provisions
   - Dispute resolution (arbitration preferred over courts)
   - IP ownership and protection clauses
   - Minimum standards and performance metrics (to enforce special permit compliance)
3. **Pilot one franchisee cautiously** — Before scaling, test the franchise model with a trusted partner in an aimag; monitor for infringement and compliance
4. **Document curriculum as trade secret** — Create written operating manual with confidentiality clauses; build dependency on franchisor for curriculum updates
5. **Consider selective expansion** — Given enforcement risks, consider company-owned branches in high-demand aimag centres rather than full franchise rollout; hybrid model reduces IP and reputational risk
