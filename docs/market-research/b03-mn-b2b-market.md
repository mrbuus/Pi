# Mongolia B2B Education Software Market Research

## Executive Summary

Mongolia's potential market for education management software is **significantly smaller than typical EdTech scaling assumptions**. The addressable market for a tutoring centre software product targeting schools and tutoring centres is approximately **100–150 identifiable institutions**, with realistic paying customers of **30–50 organisations**. Government provision of free education platforms, lack of software procurement culture, and absence of a formal tutoring centre registry create structural barriers to B2B education software sales.

### 3.3 Private School and Tutoring Centre Economics

**Private Schools**:
- 180 private schools exist; most are international or elite schools (British School of UB, American School of UB, International School of UB).
- Tuition fees at international schools: comparable to Oxford fees; clearly serve expat/elite domestic markets.
- Budget for software: likely exists, but absorbed by international headquarters or local IT budgets. Procurement is centralised with parent organisations.

**Tutoring Centres**:
- Average centre size: unknown; typical models suggest 20–150 students per centre.
- Revenue model: per-student tuition; likely 50,000–200,000 MNT (~$21–$84 USD) per student per month.
- Software budget: minimal. Most centres are sole-proprietor or small partnerships without dedicated IT budgets.

**Finding**: Ability to pay for software is **low to non-existent** for tutoring centres. Private schools have budget but purchase through international procurement channels or rely on government free alternatives (ESIS, Medle.mn).

## 5. Total Addressable Market (TAM) Calculation

### 5.1 Market Segmentation

#### Segment 1: Public Schools (705 institutions)
- **Use ESIS**: Mandatory, free government platform.
- **Alternative procurement unlikely**: Ministry policy prioritises free, government-owned systems.
- **TAM**: **0 potential customers**.

#### Segment 2: Private Schools (180 institutions)
- **Most are international schools** or elite domestic institutions.
- **Procurement independently**: Use international vendors (Managebac, SAP) or free tools (Google Classroom, ESIS).
- **Overlap with free systems**: Likely 70–80% already use ESIS or Medle.mn.
- **Addressable subset**: Domestic private schools **without international affiliation** seeking Mongolian-language, locally-supported management software.
- **Realistic TAM**: **20–40 private schools** (11–22% of total).

#### Segment 3: Private Tutoring Centres (50–300 estimated)
- **No official registry**: True count unknown; 50–300 is speculative range.
- **Most are informal or micro-scale**: Single proprietor, 10–50 students.
- **Current tools**: Excel, Facebook, paper; minimal digitisation.
- **Willingness to pay**: Low; most operate on thin margins and resist software costs.
- **Realistic penetration**: 10–20% of centres might consider paid software.
- **Realistic TAM**: **5–60 tutoring centres** (range based on 50–300 total; 10–20% adoption).

#### Segment 4: Government or Semi-Government Organisations
- **Education NGOs, training centres, corporate training departments**.
- **Limited market**: Few standalone training operations exist outside schools & tutoring centres.
- **TAM**: **5–15 organisations**.

### 5.2 TAM Summary

| Segment | Total Institutions | Realistic Addressable % | Realistic TAM | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Public Schools** | 705 | 0% | **0** | Use free ESIS; no procurement market |
| **Private Schools** | 180 | 11–22% | **20–40** | International procurement; subset seeks domestic alternatives |
| **Tutoring Centres** | 50–300 (est.) | 10–20% | **5–60** | Unknown registry; low willingness to pay |
| **Training / NGO** | ~10–20 (est.) | 25–50% | **5–15** | Minor segment |
| **Total TAM** | — | — | **30–115** | Midpoint: **60–80 paying customers** |

### 5.3 Conservative "Most Likely" TAM

Removing optimistic assumptions:
- **Tutoring centre registry uncertainty**: Use lower bound (50, not 300). 10% adoption = 5 centres.
- **Private school adoption**: International schools use existing solutions; target domestic private schools with Mongolian needs = 15–25.
- **Government/NGO**: 5 organisations.

**Most Likely TAM = 25–35 paying customers**.

### 5.4 Why the TAM is Small

1. **Free government competition**: ESIS and Medle.mn eliminate procurement demand from public schools (78% of all schools).
2. **No procurement culture**: SaaS adoption is minimal in Mongolia (22 total SaaS companies nationally).
3. **Undocumented market**: Tutoring centres are not registered, tracked, or organised into a purchasable market segment.
4. **Low ability to pay**: Tutoring centres operate on narrow margins; software spending is not a priority.
5. **International alternatives**: Private schools (if they buy) use global platforms with English support, not Mongolian-developed solutions.
6. **Concentration risk**: 80%+ of revenue would come from a handful of private schools, making the market fragile.

## 7. Conclusion & Recommendations

### 7.1 Market Reality

**Mongolia's B2B education software market is not viable for a tutoring-centre platform.**

- **Total addressable market: 25–115 potential paying customers** (most likely: **30–50**).
- **Public schools (78% of market) are closed**: ESIS is mandatory and free.
- **Tutoring centres are unregistered**: No reliable way to identify or reach them; willingness to pay is low.
- **Competition is free and government-backed**: ESIS, Medle.mn, and $130M in ADB funding ensure continued free alternatives.

### 7.2 If Pursuing This Market: Strategic Pivots

1. **Target private schools only** (20–40 realistic customers):
   - Focus on domestic private schools *not* affiliated with international organisations.
   - Emphasise Mongolian language, local teacher training, alignment with Mongolian curriculum.
   - Price aggressively ($50–200/month); expect long sales cycles.

2. **Pursue tutoring centres as a secondary market**:
   - Acknowledge uncertain market size; start with pilot in UB.
   - Offer freemium or low-cost tier ($10–30/month) to lower adoption barriers.
   - Partner with tutoring associations (if they exist) or education chambers to reach unregistered centres.

3. **Broaden beyond education management** to capture additional revenue:
   - Integrate attendance + payroll for tutoring centre staff.
   - Add parent billing / payment processing (high-value feature for centres).
   - Include marketing / student acquisition tools (centres struggle with enrollment).

4. **Position as a complement to government platforms**, not replacement:
   - Support ESIS data export so private schools can use Pi.mn as a supplementary tool.
   - Integrate with Medle.mn content where applicable.

### 7.3 Honest Assessment

**Without a clear, reachable, paying customer segment, this product has limited commercial viability in Mongolia.** The government's commitment to free platforms, coupled with low SaaS adoption and an undocumented tutoring market, make revenue projections unrealistic.

Consider whether the market is worth entering, or whether international expansion (Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Russia) offers better ROI than the Mongolian opportunity.
